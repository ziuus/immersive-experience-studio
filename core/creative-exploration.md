# Creative Exploration Protocol

## Purpose
Prevent first-association thinking. Discover a distinctive creative thesis before selecting a visual style or technology. This protocol applies to every new experience, including portfolios and product landing pages.

## Phase 1 — Frame the real problem
Write a one-page internal frame:
- Brand truth: what is demonstrably distinctive, not what sounds impressive.
- Audience: who arrives, what they already believe, what they need, what may move them.
- Business/communication objective and primary action.
- Emotional shift: what should the visitor feel before vs. after the experience?
- Narrative tension: what question, contrast, desire, friction or transformation can carry the experience?
- Constraints: content, assets, platform, budget, timeline, accessibility, device and performance.
Separate verified facts, interpretation, proposal and unknowns.

## Phase 2 — Divergent exploration
Generate at least five candidate concept seeds before narrowing. They must draw from at least four meaningfully different source domains, chosen for relevance. Possible domains include:
- Cinema: sequence, framing, montage, suspense, reveal, point of view.
- Photography: light, lens, documentary evidence, portraiture, scale, juxtaposition.
- Architecture/interiors: threshold, procession, compression/release, material, atmosphere, wayfinding.
- Theatre/performance: stage, choreography, audience participation, acts, silence.
- Fashion: identity, silhouette, material, editorial campaign, cultural codes.
- Publishing/editorial: typography, pacing, long-form narrative, art-book composition.
- Contemporary art/exhibition: installation, participation, spatial metaphor, conceptual constraint.
- Music/sound: rhythm, repetition, crescendo, motif, silence (never autoplay by default).
- Industrial/product design: tactility, assembly, precision, objecthood, affordance.
- Games/interactive fiction: agency, discovery, progression, consequence.
- Nature/science/craft/history/urban culture where genuinely relevant.

For each seed, write: working title; one-sentence thesis; source-domain connection; emotional effect; how it changes the visitor's journey; one signature scene or interaction; why it belongs to this brand; main risk. Do not start with a palette, font list or technology stack.

Do not mechanically force five unrelated styles. The goal is breadth of thought, not random novelty. Reject seeds that are only a metaphor pasted onto a conventional page.

## Phase 3 — Challenge the obvious
Explicitly identify:
1. The first three obvious concepts and why they are insufficient.
2. The strongest concept that does not literally depict the client's profession/product.
3. A restrained concept that could outperform a spectacle-heavy one.
4. A concept that would be impossible or inappropriate for this audience—and why.

Use the client’s existing visual identity and work as evidence, not as a cage. It may inform details, proof and tone without dictating the central metaphor.

## Phase 4 — Research and reference translation
When research tools are available, inspect original references from relevant disciplines. Prefer primary/original work, record creator/title/URL/date, and distinguish observed facts from interpretation. Deconstruct composition, pacing, narrative, image treatment, typography, interaction and emotional effect. Extract abstract principles; do not copy recognizable layouts, assets, copy or signature devices.

If browsing or reference inspection is unavailable, state that limitation and continue using clearly labelled conceptual reasoning. Never fabricate references or claim research was conducted.

## Phase 5 — Select three territories
Select the three strongest viable territories from the exploration. They should represent distinct creative theses and visitor experiences. Each territory must include:
- Thesis and why now/why this brand.
- Audience insight and emotional target.
- Narrative arc / visitor journey.
- Art direction: composition, typography role, image/film language, color logic, material/lighting/texture where relevant.
- Signature moment and how it is earned by the narrative.
- Interaction and motion principles, including reduced-motion behavior.
- Content and proof strategy.
- Technical approach only after the concept is clear; include low-tech alternatives.
- Feasibility, risks, trade-offs and fallback.

Do not force the territories into categories such as minimal/cinematic/3D, nor use the same concept with three skins. They may differ in medium, structure, emotional register, pacing or participation.

## Phase 6 — Originality and relevance gate
Score each territory 1–5 on:
- Brand specificity: could it belong to this brand in particular?
- Conceptual originality: is there a real idea beyond a style label?
- Emotional force: does it create a meaningful shift?
- Narrative coherence: does it shape the whole journey?
- Visual potential: can it produce memorable, art-directed frames?
- Communication: does it make the offer and action clear?
- Feasibility: can it be executed to the required quality?

Reject any territory scoring below 3 on brand specificity, conceptual originality or communication. Scores are diagnostic, not objective truth. Explain the trade-offs and recommend one without pretending certainty.

## Required output discipline
Show the exploration summary only to the degree useful; do not drown the user in raw brainstorms. Present three polished territories and a concise explanation of how the exploration moved beyond the obvious. Ask for approval at the existing gate. Do not begin a blueprint or build until approved.
