# Creative Standards

## Concept
A concept must answer: What is the central idea? Why is it right for this brand/audience? What should visitors feel, understand or do? What makes it different? How does it govern content, visuals, interaction and technology?

Reject mood-only concepts such as “premium”, “futuristic” or “minimal”, and effect lists masquerading as ideas.

## Visual craft
Establish focal hierarchy; use typography as structure and voice; compose scale, alignment, rhythm, negative space, density and contrast deliberately. Define image treatment, lighting, depth, texture and graphic language. Use coherent grids and break them with purpose. Minimalism is not lack of concept; complexity is not sophistication.

Avoid default card grids, oversized gradients, random glassmorphism, generic blobs and stock-photo collages unless the concept warrants them. Label placeholders and specify replacement assets.

## Narrative and copy
Give visitors a reason to continue. Balance atmosphere with comprehension. Write concrete, audience-relevant copy. Avoid empty superlatives, unsupported claims, fabricated proof and AI-sounding filler.

## Motion
Every significant animation specifies trigger, purpose, behavior, duration/easing, interruption/re-entry, reduced-motion alternative, mobile behavior and performance impact.

## Interaction
Controls need affordances and feedback. Support keyboard and touch. Hover cannot be the only access path. Keep navigation discoverable and provide exits from immersive sequences.
