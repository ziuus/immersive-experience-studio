# Creative Exploration Regression Benchmark

## Purpose
Detect whether the studio escapes first-association bias and creates distinct, brand-relevant territories.

## Run protocol
Use the same prompt and source material for each skill version. Record agent/model, loaded modules, tools, time, outputs and whether references were actually inspected. Do not provide hidden hints about desired concepts.

## Test A — Technical founder portfolio
Brief: Create a portfolio for a systems/AI engineer whose work includes terminal tools, AI infrastructure and graphics engineering. Goal: earn trust from technical hiring managers and invite contact. Constraint: credible, clear, responsive; no invented claims.

Failure condition: all concepts are terminals, dashboards, code, observability, performance metrics, kernels, architecture diagrams or literal depictions of engineering. Passing output must include at least one strong concept whose central creative thesis is not a direct depiction of the profession, while remaining credible and relevant.

## Test B — Temis-style product landing page
Brief: Create a landing page for a new product with limited verified product information. Goal: explain value and drive a qualified action. Constraint: no invented capabilities, testimonials or metrics; only landing-page scope.

Failure condition: generic SaaS gradient, floating dashboard, feature-card grid, or unsupported product narrative. Passing output must establish an original narrative and visual direction while clearly labelling unknown product facts.

## Test C — Consumer brand campaign
Brief: A small honey farm wants a memorable campaign and a mobile-first landing experience. Goal: communicate provenance and encourage direct enquiries. Constraint: real product/brand assets only; avoid generic bee clipart and fake sustainability claims.

Failure condition: predictable honeycomb/bee/yellow palette as the whole concept. Passing output must explore broader storytelling and art-direction possibilities without losing product truth.

## Scoring (0–2 each)
- Breadth: five concept seeds span at least four relevant domains without random padding.
- Anti-obviousness: identifies and challenges first associations.
- Thesis: concepts are ideas, not moodboards/effect lists.
- Distinctness: final three differ in visitor journey and creative mechanism.
- Specificity: each concept is grounded in audience and brand truth.
- Art direction: key frames and visual language are actionable.
- Emotional/narrative coherence: experience has a meaningful arc.
- Feasibility: technical approach follows concept and includes fallbacks.
- Communication: user understands offer and action.
- Integrity: no fabricated research, claims or test results.

Maximum 20. Pass >=16, with no zero in anti-obviousness, distinctness, specificity or integrity. A score does not replace human review.

## Regression comparison
Keep exact prompts, source content and scoring criteria stable. Compare v3.0 and v3.1 outputs side by side. Record examples of repeated concept families, not just scores. A regression is any loss of clarity, relevance, feasibility, approval discipline or factual integrity while increasing novelty.
