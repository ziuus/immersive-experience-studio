# Stage 3 — Creative Engineering

## 1. Inspect before editing

Read project configuration, routes, components, styles, assets, dependencies, and relevant business logic. Identify what must remain unchanged. Do not assume the stack or replace it without a clear reason.

## 2. Establish foundations

Implement design tokens and shared foundations for typography, color, spacing, grid, surfaces, breakpoints, focus, and motion. Keep the system proportionate to project scope.

## 3. Build the experience

Implement the approved composition and content. Preserve real functionality, routes, integrations, and data. Do not turn interactive features into static illustrations. Ensure CTAs and forms have real destinations/behavior or clearly identify what is still missing.

## 4. Motion and assets

Use the simplest suitable tools. Optimize images and fonts; lazy-load below-the-fold media where appropriate. Keep animations interruptible and performant. Support reduced motion. Avoid adding 3D/WebGL unless it is central to the concept and justified by the user experience.

## 5. Validate

Run relevant build, lint, type, and test commands. Use a browser/preview when available. Inspect the actual rendered page at desktop and mobile sizes. Test navigation, forms, buttons, menus, and interactive elements. Check console/runtime errors and overflow.

## 6. Critique and iterate

Compare the rendered result to the approved creative direction and blueprint. Identify the three most consequential visual/UX weaknesses, fix them, and inspect again. Continue until major defects are resolved or explain why they cannot be.

## 7. Report honestly

Summarize what changed, what was preserved, what was tested, what could not be tested, and any remaining limitations. Never claim screenshot/browser testing unless it actually occurred.
