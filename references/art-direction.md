# Art Direction Reference

## Composition
- Establish a clear focal point and deliberate reading path.
- Use scale, asymmetry, rhythm, layering, and negative space intentionally.
- Vary section composition when it strengthens the narrative.
- Avoid repetitive card grids, arbitrary containers, and identical section templates.
- Design around the content and concept, not around a preselected component library.

## Typography
- Select typefaces for brand fit, language coverage, readability, and licensing.
- Define a clear scale and hierarchy for display, headings, body, labels, and metadata.
- Use expressive display typography only when it supports the identity.
- Control line length, line breaks, weight, and contrast.

## Color and materials
- Establish semantic roles for backgrounds, surfaces, text, accents, and states.
- Use contrast intentionally and verify readability.
- Treat gradients, grain, glass, blur, glow, and texture as materials with a reason—not defaults.

## Imagery
- Prefer original, relevant, art-directed assets over generic stock.
- Specify image subject, framing, lighting, color treatment, and role.
- Avoid decorative imagery that competes with the message.
- Do not use unlicensed or misleading assets.

## Originality
- References are evidence of craft, not templates to reproduce.
- Identify the specific quality being referenced and reinterpret it for this brand.
- Ask whether the design could be mistaken for a generic AI template. If yes, strengthen the concept and visual signature.
