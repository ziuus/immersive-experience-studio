# Originality Gate — Anti-Obviousness Review

Run before presenting Gate 1 territories and again after prototype inspection.

## Failure signals
- Concept is a literal visual translation of the client's occupation, product category or technology stack.
- Concept is a list of effects, libraries or visual adjectives rather than a thesis.
- Three options share the same narrative and interaction model with palette/font changes.
- Reference is recognizable because the design copies its signature layout, image, copy or motion.
- A technical feature is included mainly to demonstrate technical ability.
- Visitor must learn an unfamiliar navigation metaphor before understanding the offer.
- Emotional language is unsupported by actual content, pacing or visual decisions.

## Challenge questions
1. What is the less-obvious interpretation of the brief?
2. What creative discipline outside the client's industry changed the direction?
3. What would be lost if all advanced technology were removed?
4. What is the one frame a visitor would remember, and why?
5. Is the experience distinctive to the brand while still immediately understandable?
6. Is there a simpler execution with greater emotional or communicative force?
7. What should be removed?

## Decision
- PASS: clear thesis, brand specificity, meaningful differentiation, communication and feasibility.
- REVISE: strong potential but one or more dimensions remain generic or unclear.
- REJECT: mostly aesthetic styling, literal metaphor, gimmick or imitation.

Record rationale and evidence. Never convert the score into a claim that the work is objectively award-winning.
