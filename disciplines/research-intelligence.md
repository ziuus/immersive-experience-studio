# Research & Intelligence

Research should sharpen the creative thesis and reduce risk, not become endless inspiration browsing.

## Investigate
Brand/product facts; audience and context; category conventions; competitors; creative references; current technical capabilities, APIs, licenses and browser support.

Prefer primary sources for product facts and technical documentation. Design discovery sources include Awwwards (https://www.awwwards.com/), The FWA (https://thefwa.com/), SiteInspire (https://www.siteinspire.com/), Godly (https://godly.website/) and It's Nice That (https://www.itsnicethat.com/). These are starting points, not endorsements. Inspect original work.

## Deconstruct each reference
Record title/creator/URL/access date; purpose; central idea; composition; narrative; motion/interaction; verified technical observations; strengths/weaknesses; transferable abstract principle; what must not be copied; relevance.

Do not copy distinctive layouts, copy, illustrations, code or brand identity. Translate principles into a new solution.

## Competitive analysis
Compare positioning, promise, audience, proof, content hierarchy, interaction, visual conventions and unmet expectations. Avoid unsupported claims such as “no one else does this”.

## Integrity labels
- Observed: directly visible/stated.
- Inferred: interpretation with rationale.
- Proposed: recommendation.
- Unknown: not established.

Cite sources and access dates for volatile facts when tools allow. Never invent browsing, user research, analytics or tests. Stop when more research is unlikely to change decisions.
